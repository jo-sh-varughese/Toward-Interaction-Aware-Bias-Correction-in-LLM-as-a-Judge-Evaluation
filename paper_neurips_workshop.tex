\begin{abstract}
Whether an LLM judge yields a valid evaluation depends not only on
its accuracy but on how its error profile composes with the
debiasing pipeline around it and the decisions its scores feed
downstream. We study one such composition: pipelines
correct position and verbosity bias \emph{sequentially}, assuming
the two add. We show this assumption is (a) consequential, (b)
checkable without human labels, and (c) currently unchecked.
\textbf{Consequential:} if position and verbosity bias interact
($\beta_{PV}\neq0$), a pipeline that judges each pair once and
\emph{subtracts} a fitted position term leaves a residual of
$\tfrac12|\beta_{PV}|$ that no ordering removes; \emph{averaging}
over both orders removes it on the log-odds scale (up to a Jensen
gap)---so which deployed designs are exposed is nameable, and a
residual of this size shifts leaderboard order and a measurable
fraction of RLHF preference labels. \textbf{Checkable:} the
\textbf{Swap-Symmetry Interaction Test} (SSIT) is a
difference-in-differences over the four position$\times$verbosity
swap cells; its readout $\hat\imath_{PV}$ is a difference of four
cell means, estimating the population interaction in win-rate points
with \emph{no human labels and no model of the judge}. It needs a
dedicated collection (${\approx}8\times$ one evaluation's cost) but
no preference labels. We give a power analysis, decision procedure,
and deployment-disclosure checklist. For $1\times$-budget
pipelines with no labels, \textbf{Interaction-Aware Swap Correction}
(IASC) shrinks each position-coupled bias term toward zero---a
floor, not a fix. \textbf{Unchecked:} a pilot on seven open judges
over six families ($\approx$20 pairs each, no labels) finds
$\hat\imath_{PV}>0$ on six ($+0.07$ to $+0.17$; wild cluster
bootstrap $p\!\leq\!0.03$), AIA-significant on four; the seventh
picks the stronger response on every pair, so SSIT correctly returns
$0$ (the ``too-easy stimulus'' regime). For four re-collected
judges, a length-only \emph{padding} manipulation keeps the pooled
interaction (at half the magnitude) but splits by judge---growing
for one, holding for two, vanishing for one whose $P{\times}V$ was
really position$\times$style---so a paraphrase probe alone can
mislead about the mechanism. Code, data, and a valid-CI (PPI)
variant released.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

LLM-as-a-judge evaluation~\cite{zheng2023judging,gu2024survey}
supplies reward signals for RLHF~\cite{sharma2023sycophancy} and
determines leaderboard rankings~\cite{dubois2024length}. Three
systematic biases are well documented---positional ($\BP$),
verbosity ($\BV$), self-preference ($\BS$)---each with a published
mitigation~\cite{zheng2023judging,dubois2024length,panickssery2024llm}.
Deployed pipelines apply them one after another: swap positions and
average, then length-control, then diversify the panel.

\textbf{The gap.} No prior work asks whether this chaining is
\emph{valid}---whether the biases compose additively---since the
natural check, measuring the interaction against human preference
labels, defeats the purpose of an automatic judge. The validity
question is not ``how accurate is the judge'' but ``how does its
error compose with the pipeline'': a judge can be well-calibrated in
isolation yet leave a systematic residual once its corrections are
chained. This paper treats that check when there are no labels:
(1) \textbf{Which pipelines are exposed} (\S\ref{sec:theory},
Prop.~\ref{prop:residual}, Cor.~\ref{cor:composite})---the answer to
a standing question, \emph{does position-swap averaging fully
debias pairwise evaluation?} Only on the log-odds scale: a
$1\times$ subtract-a-term pipeline still leaves
$\tfrac12|\beta_{PV}|$; a $2\times$ swap-average cancels it there
but not on non-position pairs ($V{\times}S$, \dots); joint
estimation is exact. (2) \textbf{Downstream cost}
(\S\ref{sec:downstream}): an uncorrected interaction of the
magnitude our worked example suggests reorders adjacent leaderboard
models and mislabels an estimable fraction of length-mismatched
RLHF comparisons. (3) \textbf{The check} (SSIT, \S\ref{sec:ssit},
Prop.~\ref{prop:ssit}): a difference-in-differences over the four
$P{\times}V$ swap cells, \emph{design-based}---no human labels, no
model of how the judge decides, robust across six decision rules
(\S\ref{sec:ssit-robust})---at a dedicated collection cost of
${\approx}8\times$ one evaluation, with a power analysis for sizing
it (\S\ref{sec:power}). (4) \textbf{A $1\times$-budget correction}
(IASC, \S\ref{sec:iasc}, Prop.~\ref{prop:iasc}): shrinks each
position-coupled bias term toward zero without labels; a floor, not
a fix. (5) \textbf{A seven-judge pilot, a decision procedure, and a
deployment-disclosure checklist}
(\S\ref{sec:pilot}--\ref{sec:discussion}): $\hat\imath_{PV}>0$ on
six of seven open judges across six families, correctly null on the
seventh; a length-only re-test of four judges shows the interaction
persists pooled but its mechanism (length vs.\ paraphrase-style) is
judge-specific.
A valid-CI version via prediction-powered
inference~\cite{ppi_ranking2026,eif_judge2026} is in
Appendix~\ref{app:iappi-est}; self-preference and other factor pairs
use the same contrast (Appendix~\ref{app:pairs}).

\textbf{Scope.} The only non-simulation numbers are the propositions
and the pilot (\S\ref{sec:pilot}), which demonstrates the
diagnostic, not judge behaviour. Simulations use \emph{stipulated}
interaction magnitudes to check estimator recovery and power and to
bound downstream cost, not to size the effect for real
judges---that is what the pre-registered study
(\S\ref{sec:discussion}) is for.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\textbf{Single biases, measured in isolation.} Position, verbosity,
and self-preference bias each have a published
mitigation~\cite{zheng2023judging,wang2023fair,shi2024position,saito2023verbosity,dubois2024length,panickssery2024llm,selfpref2024};
taxonomies and audits~\cite{ye2024justice,gu2024survey,li2024generation,thakur2024judging}
keep the one-bias-at-a-time convention. Length-controlled
AlpacaEval~\cite{dubois2024length} removes verbosity as a
\emph{marginal} confounder by regression---the regime
Prop.~\ref{prop:residual} targets.

\textbf{Multi-bias measurement.} The closest empirical
work~\cite{mitigation2026} evaluates nine debiasing strategies over
five judges and four bias types: \emph{style} bias (markdown vs.\
plain prose) dominates---up to $0.76$---position bias
\emph{in isolation} is small ($\leq0.04$), and verbosity bias
sign-flips by family (concise-preferring for Claude,
length-preferring for the rest), which our pilot reproduces. It
measures each bias against human labels on a fixed panel but does
not ask whether corrections \emph{compose}---a small main effect can
still carry a consequential interaction, and our worked example
measures $P\times$(paraphrase), overlapping that dominant style
axis~\cite{wu2023style}. Our contribution is orthogonal: a
\emph{label-free} test run \emph{per judge} at deployment scale,
where gold labels are missing. Per-instance
detectors~\cite{biasdetector2025} act on several biases at once but
give no population-level guarantee.

\textbf{Prediction-powered inference for evaluation.}
PPI/PPI++~\cite{angelopoulos2023ppi,angelopoulos2024ppipp} combines
a small gold set with a large model-labelled set for valid CIs;
\cite{ppi_ranking2026} brings this to LLM-based ranking, and
\cite{eif_judge2026} relates PPI-style estimators to the efficient
influence function and when they beat measurement-error corrections.
All learn a rectifier $\mu(\hat Y)$ of the judge score
alone---Prop.~\ref{prop:coverage} shows this is insufficient under
interaction-induced cell heterogeneity, and a factorial rectifier
$\mu(\hat Y,P,V,PV)$ restores validity (Appendix~\ref{app:iappi-est}).

\section{Sequential Correction Leaves a Residual}
\label{sec:theory}

Model the judge's choice as logistic in binary bias
indicators---position $P$, verbosity $V$, self-preference $S$---and
a quality term:
\begin{equation}
  \logit P(Y{=}A) = \gamma(Q) + \beta_P P + \beta_V V + \beta_S S
  + \beta_{PV}PV + \beta_{PS}PS + \beta_{VS}VS + \beta_{PVS}PVS .
  \label{eq:full}
\end{equation}
Sequential correction (SC) assumes \emph{additive independence}
(AIA): all interaction terms are zero. It is a two-stage pipeline:
estimate $\hat\beta_P$ by marginal regression of $Y$ on $P$ and
subtract $\hat\beta_P P$; then estimate $\hat\beta_V$ from
position-matched data (in swap pipelines, the reference stratum
$P{=}0$) and subtract $\hat\beta_V V$.

\begin{proposition}[SC residual under interaction]
\label{prop:residual}
Under~\eqref{eq:full} restricted to $P,V$, balanced
($P{\perp}V$, $\Pr{=}\tfrac12$), $\beta_{PV}\neq0$:
$\hat\beta_P^{\mathrm{marg}}\xrightarrow{p}\beta_P+\tfrac12\beta_{PV}$
(omitted-variable bias), $\hat\beta_V\xrightarrow{p}\beta_V$, and
after both corrections the $(P{=}1,V{=}1)$ cell retains a residual
log-odds
$\Delta_{\mathrm{SC}}=(\beta_P{+}\beta_V{+}\beta_{PV})
-(\beta_P{+}\tfrac12\beta_{PV})-\beta_V=\tfrac12\beta_{PV}$.
Any symmetric variant of the pipeline leaves an uncorrected term of
order $\tfrac12|\beta_{PV}|$ somewhere in the $2{\times}2$ table.
\end{proposition}

\begin{corollary}[Does swap-averaging fully debias? Which pipelines are exposed]
\label{cor:composite}
The residual depends on \emph{how} position bias is removed.
(i) \textbf{Subtract-a-fitted-term} ($1\times$ judging, then remove
$\hat\beta_P P$ from the score, AlpacaEval-style regression
debiasing): exposed, residual $\tfrac12\beta_{PV}$.
(ii) \textbf{Swap-average} (judge both orders, average the score):
the $(2P{-}1)$-odd terms---$\beta_P$ \emph{and}
$\beta_{PV}(2P{-}1)V$---cancel \emph{in the log-odds}. Pipelines
average the probability/score, and
$\tfrac12[\sigma(x){+}\sigma(y)]\neq\sigma(\tfrac12(x{+}y))$, so a
second-order Jensen term of size $O(\beta_{PV}\cdot|\delta{+}\beta_V V|)$
survives; it is small when the two orders have similar baseline
rates and $0$ at $\beta_{PV}{=}0$. V$\times$S and other non-position
pairs are untouched by swap-averaging.
(iii) \textbf{Joint estimation} ($P{+}V{+}PV$ regression): exact.
Case (i) needs SSIT to decide whether to act; case (ii) needs it for
the non-position pairs and, if the baseline-rate gap is large, for
P$\times$V too.
\end{corollary}

\noindent The algebra is textbook omitted-variable
bias~\cite{angrist2009}; the contribution is pinning \emph{which}
pipelines this exposes---swap-averaging debiases $P{\times}V$ on the
log-odds scale but not the surface-form/self-preference pairs, and
subtract-a-term pipelines not at all. Whether it bites for your
judge still requires measuring $\beta_{PV}$---\S\ref{sec:ssit}.

\section{Downstream: What an Uncorrected Interaction Costs}
\label{sec:downstream}

Write $\imath_{PV}$ for the interaction on the \emph{win-rate}
scale---the probability-scale image of $\beta_{PV}$
(\S\ref{sec:theory}), the quantity a debiased pipeline should drive
to zero. An uncorrected $\imath_{PV}$ biases the measured win rate on
length-mismatched comparisons by up to $\imath_{PV}$ points, flowing
into whatever those win rates feed
(\texttt{sim\_ranking\_impact.py}). We take
$\imath_{PV}{=}0.15$---within our pilot's $+0.07$--$+0.17$ range
(\S\ref{sec:pilot})---as a running figure.

\textbf{Leaderboards.} In a constructed six-model leaderboard
(adjacent true win rate ${\approx}51\%$), a judge carrying
$\imath_{PV}{=}0.15$ inverts the ranking under no correction and
under sequential correction alike (Kendall $\tau{=}+0.20$; worst
model above best), since the differential lives entirely in
$\beta_{PV}$. Log-odds swap-averaging and joint estimation restore
$\tau{=}1.0$; label-free IASC recovers $\tau{=}0.87$. The
construction is adversarial, so the absolute drop is an upper
bound, but the ordering of \emph{corrections} is stable across a
$4\times$ sweep of the bias magnitude (Appendix~\ref{app:ranksweep}).

\textbf{Preference data.} On the affected cell the judge's
pick-target probability is shifted by up to $\imath_{PV}$ relative
to the quality-only preference: if quality preferences cluster near
$50/50$ this sets the labelled winner by the interaction rather than
quality for $26$--$44\%$ of that cell at $\imath_{PV}{=}0.15$
($9$--$26\%$ at $\imath_{PV}{=}0.05$--$0.15$); halve those rates for
a corpus with a length gap on only a fraction $f$ of comparisons.
These labels compound into model weights through
RLHF/DPO~\cite{sharma2023sycophancy}---and, because verbose
responses are favoured on the very cell where position bias is
cancelled, in a direction that rewards verbosity.

\section{A Label-Free Test for the Interaction}
\label{sec:ssit}

Run every pair through all four $(P,V)$ swap cells. Swapping the two
responses maps $P\!\to\!1{-}P$ and (pick-target)$\to$(pick-other),
so the judge's dependence on position must be \emph{odd} in $P$:
write it $\beta_P(2P{-}1)$, with the interaction $\beta_{PV}(2P{-}1)V$.
The pooled log-odds of picking the target is then
\begin{equation}
  \logit\pi_1(P,V)=\delta+\beta_P(2P{-}1)+\beta_V V
    +\beta_{PV}(2P{-}1)V,
  \label{eq:swapmodel}
\end{equation}
$\pi_1{=}\Pr(\text{pick target})$, $\delta$ the unknown per-pair
quality gap.

\begin{proposition}[Swap-symmetry identity]
\label{prop:ssit}
The difference-in-differences of the four cell log-odds,
$\Delta_{\mathrm{SS}}=
[\logit\pi_1(1,1)-\logit\pi_1(0,1)]
-[\logit\pi_1(1,0)-\logit\pi_1(0,0)]$,
equals $2\beta_{PV}$ and does not depend on $\delta$, $\beta_P$, or
$\beta_V$ (Appendix~\ref{app:ssit}).
\end{proposition}

We call the plug-in the \textbf{Swap-Symmetry Interaction Test}
(SSIT), with two readouts and it matters which you trust: (a)
$\hat\imath_{PV}$, the \textbf{same DiD on the probability
scale}---a difference of the four within-pair cell means,
$[\bar r_{11}-\bar r_{01}]-[\bar r_{10}-\bar r_{00}]$,
\emph{design-based}, estimating the population interaction in
win-rate points under \emph{any} judge decision rule (no link,
additivity, or homogeneity needed; \eqref{eq:swapmodel} plays no
role); and (b) $\hat\beta_{PV}$, the log-odds coefficient from the
saturated 8-cell fit, which \emph{does} use \eqref{eq:swapmodel} and
attenuates over heterogeneous $\delta$---report it only as a rough
secondary number, with $\geq3$ samples/cell. No gold labels enter
either (a small gold set only adds a CI on the debiased win rate
itself, Appendix~\ref{app:iappi-est}); the same contrast on other
factor pairs gives $\imath_{PS},\imath_{VS}$.

\textbf{Cost.} SSIT is label-free but not free: each calibration pair
is judged in all four $(P,V)$ cells at $\geq2$ samples
($\approx$8$\times$ the cost of scoring it once) plus a verbosity
manipulation a debiasing pipeline does not otherwise produce---a
dedicated experiment on a calibration subset, cheaper than human
preferences but not a free rider on the two-order swap debiasing
already runs, and needing no preference labels.

\subsection{What does $\hat\imath_{PV}$ actually assume?}
\label{sec:ssit-robust}

Almost nothing. We plant a judge, compute its \emph{true} population
$\imath_{PV}$ by high-$N$ Monte Carlo, and check whether SSIT
recovers it from $60$-pair panels with no labels, across six
decision rules---one \emph{out of family}: heavy-tailed
(Student-$t$) quality perception, a hard confidence threshold, no
link function at all.

\subsection{How large a check do you need?}
\label{sec:power}

$\hat\imath_{PV}=\bar d=\tfrac1n\sum_k d_k$ is a sample mean of the
per-pair DiDs $d_k$, so by the CLT
$\bar d\approx\mathcal N(\imath_{PV},\sigma_d^2/n)$ with
$\sigma_d^2=\mathrm{Var}(d_k)$, and the two-sided level-$\alpha$
wild-cluster test has power

\vspace{-0.6em}
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{power}\ \approx\
  \Phi\!\Bigl(\tfrac{|\imath_{PV}|\sqrt n}{\sigma_d}-z_{1-\alpha/2}\Bigr),
  \qquad
  n_{80\%}\ \approx\ \frac{7.8\,\sigma_d^2}{\imath_{PV}^2}.
  \label{eq:power}
\end{equation}

\noindent This is \emph{a priori}: it needs only $\sigma_d$ (a
between-pair term plus a sampling term $\propto 1/s$), pinned from a
$\leq10$-pair pre-run or borrowed from a similar judge. A decisive
judge ($\sigma_d\approx0.25$, \S\ref{sec:pilot}) needs $n\gtrsim22$
for a $0.15$ effect; mid-range ($\sigma_d\approx0.4$) needs
$n\gtrsim55$. Table~\ref{tab:power}'s wild-bootstrap simulation
tracks \eqref{eq:power}, mildly conservative below $n{=}30$ (it
estimates $\sigma_d$ rather than knowing it).

observed $\mathrm{SD}(d_k)$ (realized-power column of
Table~\ref{tab:pilot}).

\section{IASC: A Partial Label-Free Correction}
\label{sec:iasc}

The swap contrasts that identify $\beta_{PV}$ also identify
$\beta_P$ (and $\beta_{PS},\beta_{PVS}$ with a third factor)---every
coefficient whose monomial carries $(2P{-}1)$, the
\emph{position-coupled subspace}. Reading these off a
swap-augmented calibration subset and subtracting them gives
\textbf{Interaction-Aware Swap Correction}:
\begin{equation}
  \hat s_i = \sigma\!\bigl(\logit\hat Y_i
    - \hat\beta_P(2P_i{-}1) - \hat\beta_{PV}(2P_i{-}1)V_i\bigr).
  \label{eq:iasc}
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[IASC shrinks each position-coupled term]
\label{prop:iasc}
Fitting a common slope across pairs with heterogeneous $\delta_k$
attenuates each swap-cell coefficient,
$\hat\beta_P\xrightarrow{p}\lambda_P\beta_P$ and
$\hat\beta_{PV}\xrightarrow{p}\lambda_{PV}\beta_{PV}$ with
$\lambda_P,\lambda_{PV}\in(0,1]$ increasing to $1$ as
$\mathrm{Var}(\delta_k)\to0$ (Appendix~\ref{app:ssit}). Then
\eqref{eq:iasc} gives $\E[\logit\hat s_i\mid k]=\delta_k
+(1{-}\lambda_P)\beta_P(2P_i{-}1)+\beta_V V_i+\beta_S S_i
+(1{-}\lambda_{PV})\beta_{PV}(2P_i{-}1)V_i$: a positive fraction of
each position-coupled term is removed, sign preserved, and
$\beta_V,\beta_S$ untouched.
\end{proposition}

\noindent Two honest caveats. (i) $\lambda_P$ and $\lambda_{PV}$
need not be equal, so a cell can retain oppositely-signed
main-effect and interaction residuals. (ii) The guarantee is on the
\emph{log-odds}; after $\sigma(\cdot)$ the corrected rate need not
lie between raw and swap-averaged---on the pilot it moves
$0.93\to0.98$ past the $2\times$ swap-average of $0.95$
(Table~\ref{tab:iasc}), a Jensen overshoot, so IASC is a heuristic
floor, not a guaranteed win-rate improvement.

\noindent\textbf{When to use it.} IASC fits one situation: judge
each pair \emph{once}, no gold labels, wanting back part of what
$2\times$ swap-averaging would remove---it removes $\approx60$--$80\%$
of each position-coupled log-odds term on quality-matched pairs,
less otherwise (Table~\ref{tab:ssit_robust}), for one extra swapped
pass. With a gold set, use the factorial-rectifier estimator instead
(Appendix~\ref{app:coverage}).

\section{Pilot: Seven Judges, Six Families}
\label{sec:results}
\label{sec:pilot}

We ran SSIT gold-free on seven open judges spanning six model
families---\texttt{gpt-oss}-20B/120B (Groq), \texttt{gemma}
(Google AI Studio), \texttt{qwen3-27b} (Groq \texttt{qwen/qwen3.8-27b}),
\texttt{allam-2-7b} (Groq), \texttt{mistral-small}, \texttt{command-r}
(Cohere)---$\approx$20 pairs each, $1$--$2$ samples/cell
(Table~\ref{tab:pilot}). $n{\approx}20$ is below what \S\ref{sec:power}
recommends for a mid-range judge, and $V$ is a paraphrase (below), so
we read this as a multi-judge feasibility test, not a magnitude
estimate.

\begin{table}[htbp]

\textbf{Six of seven show it; the seventh is unchallenged.}
$\hat\imath_{PV}$ is positive on every judge except \texttt{command-r}
($+0.07$ to $+0.17$), AIA-significant on four judges across three
families (\texttt{gpt-oss}-20B/120B, \texttt{gemma},
\texttt{qwen3-27b}; wild $p\leq0.026$, both bootstrap CIs excluding
0), and
positive-but-marginal on \texttt{mistral-small} and
\texttt{allam-2-7b} (wild $p\approx0.033$, CI touching 0; neither
survives Bonferroni). The detecting judges have
$\mathrm{SD}(d_k)\approx0.2$--$0.3$, affording $60$--$90\%$ power at
$n{\approx}20$ for a $0.15$ effect via \eqref{eq:power}---expected
detections, not luck.
\texttt{command-r} picks the stronger response on all $20$ pairs, so
$\hat\imath_{PV}{=}0$ exactly---not because it is unbiased but
because our $r_2$ responses are visibly weaker, the regime where
Table~\ref{tab:ssit_robust}'s ``bias fades when the target is
clearly better'' row applies and where SSIT correctly returns null.
It is why the pre-registered design uses quality-matched pairs. The
$\hat b_V$ sign flip persists (concise-preferring \texttt{gemma}
vs.\ length-preferring rest), so the interaction is not one model's
length taste.

\textbf{A length-only probe separates position$\times$length from
position$\times$style.} Our default $V$ rewrites $r_1$ into a longer
form, so it entangles length with rephrasing---and
\cite{mitigation2026} reports that \emph{style} bias, not position
bias, dominates. We re-collect four judges with a \emph{padding-only}
$V$ that keeps $r_1$ verbatim and appends redundant restatement of
points already made (\texttt{make\_padded\_v.py}), so $V$ toggles
length alone. Pooled, the interaction persists
($\hat\imath_{PV}{=}{+}0.06$ excl.\ \texttt{command-r}, wild
$p{=}0.003$), about half the paraphrase-$V$ magnitude---but the
mechanism is judge-specific: \texttt{mistral-small} \emph{grows}
($+0.075\!\to\!+0.10$, wild $p{=}0.003$), \texttt{allam-2-7b} holds
marginally ($+0.09\!\to\!+0.075$, $p{=}0.07$), \texttt{command-r}
stays $0$, and \texttt{qwen3-27b}'s interaction \emph{vanishes}
($+0.12$, $p{=}0.0008\to-0.02$, CI $[-0.18,+0.15]$, $p{=}0.85$): for
that judge the paraphrase-driven component carried $P{\times}V$ and
the length-only signal is null (with the higher verdict-drop caveat
of Appendix~\ref{app:validation})---so paraphrase and padding-only
$V$ can give opposite answers for the same judge, which is why the
pre-registered protocol (Appendix~\ref{app:prereg}) mandates
length-only $V$.

\textbf{What it does not establish.} An effect size for a deployed
judge: $n{\approx}20$, no frontier judge, padding-only re-test on
four of seven. \cite{mitigation2026}'s $\leq0.04$ position bias is
the \emph{main effect}; ours is the interaction, which
\S\ref{sec:theory}'s residual argument says a sequential pipeline
leaves behind regardless of mechanism. Self-preference and
$V{\times}S$ (Appendix~\ref{app:pairs}) are weaker and, for $S$,
confounded; $\approx$15\% of \texttt{gemma} verdicts are dropped
(Appendix~\ref{app:validation}).

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}
\label{sec:limitations}

The additivity assumption behind chained bias correction is now
cheap to check and cheap to act on. We close with the operational
version.

\textbf{Decision procedure.} For an evaluation that reports a
sequentially position-corrected win rate: (1) if the pipeline
\emph{swap-averages} the log-odds and the two orders have similar
baseline rates, $P{\times}V$ is handled (Cor.~\ref{cor:composite});
run SSIT only for the non-position pairs. (2) Else, run SSIT per
judge on enough pairs for $\geq\!80\%$ power at your smallest effect
of concern (Table~\ref{tab:power}: $\approx$50--100 at $2$
samples/cell for a $0.15$ effect, fewer for a decisive judge);
report $\hat\imath_{PV}$ with a wild-cluster CI \emph{and} the
realized power from the observed $\mathrm{SD}(d_k)$. (3) CI excludes
$0\Rightarrow$ act (step 4); CI contains $0$ \emph{and} realized
power $\geq\!0.8\Rightarrow$ sequential correction is defensible for
effects above your threshold; CI contains $0$ \emph{and} power
$<\!0.8\Rightarrow$ \textbf{inconclusive}: collect more
pairs/samples, do not treat as a pass. (4) Act: switch to log-odds
swap-averaging or joint $P{+}V{+}PV$ estimation; if stuck at
$1\times$ with a gold set, use the factorial-rectifier estimator
(Appendix~\ref{app:coverage}); if $1\times$ and no labels, apply
IASC as a floor.

\textbf{Deployment-disclosure checklist.} A judge gating training or
ranking decisions should ship an evaluation-pipeline
disclosure---a model-card analog---and $P{\times}V$ is one line of
it. Disclose: (1) how position bias is removed (subtractive /
log-odds-averaged / joint); (2) $\hat\imath_{PV}$, and the relevant
non-position pairs, with a wild-cluster CI and realized power; (3)
whether the verbosity probe was length-only or paraphrase; (4) what
the scores feed upstream/downstream---the same four questions a
\emph{reviewer} should ask. This checklist is deliberately silent on
training-data provenance and human-validation agreement: SSIT's
value is precisely that it needs neither, but a full disclosure
should still report them separately when available.

\textbf{Pre-registered full study} (Appendix~\ref{app:prereg}):
$\geq\!8$ judges, $\geq\!5$ families \emph{including frontier
judges}, adequately powered, padding-only $V$.

\textbf{Limitations.} Every magnitude outside the propositions and
pilot $\hat\imath_{PV}$ is simulation-based, not measured; the
pilot's scope limits ($n{\approx}20$, no frontier judge, a
four-judge padding check, and \texttt{command-r}'s null reflecting
an easy stimulus rather than an unbiased judge) are noted in
\S\ref{sec:pilot}. IASC and
\IAPPI\ are post-hoc, post-registration additions (\IAPPI\ is
post-stratified PPI); SSIT's contribution is label-free, per-judge
swap symmetry, plus the exposure taxonomy, power analysis, and
decision procedure, not the estimator.

\section{Detection Protocol}
\label{app:prereg}
The interaction-detection endpoint and its decision rule were fixed
in advance of the pilot: primary endpoint $\hat\imath_{PV}$ (the
probability-scale swap-cell DiD) per judge and pooled; decision rule
``$\hat\imath_{PV}$ 95\% CI excludes 0 and
$|\hat\imath_{PV}|>0.02\Rightarrow$ sequential correction
inadequate.'' SSIT is the estimator for that endpoint.

\noindent\textbf{Target design for the full study.}
\begin{itemize}\itemsep1pt
\item $\geq 8$ judges over $\geq 5$ model families, \emph{including}
  frontier deployed judges (GPT-4-class, Claude, Gemini) alongside
  open models.
\item Stimuli quality-matched by an independent rater
  ($|\Delta\text{quality}|<0.1$); $\geq 50$ pairs $\times$ 4 task
  types.
\item Verbosity manipulated by \emph{padding} $r_1$ with redundant
  but on-topic sentences (length only), not paraphrase expansion,
  to unconfound length from rephrasing.
\item All eight $(P,V,S)$ swap cells, $\geq 3$ samples/cell;
  self-preference cells use a held-out generator, not the judge's
  own output, to unconfound $S$ from length/style.
\item Inference: wild-cluster-bootstrap CI (robust with few
  clusters); Bonferroni over the three factor pairs.
\end{itemize}
The reported pilot is a small initial slice that omits the frontier
judges, the padding manipulation, and the multiplicity control.

IASC and \IAPPI\ were developed after the initial two-judge
collection and are corrections evaluated \emph{post hoc} on the same
frozen data---not part of the pre-registered analysis. We flag them
as such throughout.

\section{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:ssit}}
\label{app:ssit}
Under $\logit\pi_1(P,V)=\delta+\beta_P(2P{-}1)+\beta_V V
+\beta_{PV}(2P{-}1)V$:
$\logit\pi_1(1,1)-\logit\pi_1(0,1)=2\beta_P+2\beta_{PV}$ and
$\logit\pi_1(1,0)-\logit\pi_1(0,0)=2\beta_P$; their difference is
$2\beta_{PV}$. Every term in $\delta$ and $\beta_V$ (which do not
depend on $P$) cancels in the inner differences, and $\beta_P$
cancels in the outer difference. \hfill$\square$

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Proposition~\ref{prop:iasc} (IASC shrinkage).}
The four cell log-odds identify $\beta_P$ (inner difference
$=2\beta_P$) and $\beta_{PV}$ (DiD $=2\beta_{PV}$) free of
$\delta,\beta_V$. The plug-in fits a \emph{single} slope pair across
pairs whose $\delta_k$ vary; a pooled logistic slope under an
omitted heterogeneous intercept attenuates,
$\hat\beta_\bullet\xrightarrow{p}\lambda_\bullet\beta_\bullet$ with
$\lambda_\bullet\in(0,1]$ and $\lambda_\bullet\to1$ as
$\mathrm{Var}\,\delta\to0$ (mixed-logit / Jensen). Substituting into
\eqref{eq:iasc},
$\E[\logit\hat s_i\mid k]=\delta_k
+(1{-}\lambda_P)\beta_P(2P_i{-}1)+\beta_V V_i+\beta_S S_i
+(1{-}\lambda_{PV})\beta_{PV}(2P_i{-}1)V_i$: a positive fraction of
each position-coupled term is removed, sign preserved.
$\lambda_P$ and $\lambda_{PV}$ need not be equal, so a cell can
retain oppositely-signed main-effect and interaction residuals; and
the shrinkage guarantee is on the logit scale---after $\sigma$ the
Jensen gap can push the corrected pick rate outside
$[\text{raw},\text{swap-avg}]$. At $\lambda_P=\lambda_{PV}=1$
(matched calibration pairs) IASC coincides with $2\times$
swap-averaging in the log-odds. \hfill$\square$

\section{Optional CI Companion: the Factorial Rectifier}
\label{app:coverage}
\label{app:iappi-est}
\label{app:valid}

This appendix is a companion, not a core contribution: it says how
to attach a \emph{valid confidence interval} to a debiased win rate
when a small gold set \emph{is} available. Reference implementation:
\texttt{ia\_ppi.py}.

Prediction-powered inference (PPI++~\cite{angelopoulos2024ppipp,
eif_judge2026}) debiases a large judge-labelled set $\mathcal{E}$
against a small gold set $\mathcal{C}$ using a rectifier
$\mu(\hat y)=\E[H\mid\hat Y{=}\hat y]$.

\begin{proposition}[Marginal PPI under-covers under cell shift]
\label{prop:coverage}
With $g(\hat y,c)=\E[H\mid\hat Y{=}\hat y,c]-\mu(\hat y)$ and
$\pi_c,\pi_c'$ the cell frequencies in $\mathcal{C},\mathcal{E}$,
$\hat\theta_{\mathrm{PPI}}$ has asymptotic bias
$\sum_c(\pi_c'-\pi_c)\E[g\mid c]$ with
$\E[g\mid c]=\tfrac14 s_c\beta_{PV}+O(\beta_{PV}^2)$; the coverage
loss is order (bias$/$SE). The \emph{factorial} rectifier
$\mu(\hat y,P,V,PV)$---fit on $\mathcal{C}$ or seeded from SSIT---sets
$g\equiv0$; the resulting estimator is $\sqrt{\Ncal}$-asymptotically
normal with a sandwich variance, and its point estimate equals the
joint $P{+}V{+}PV$ plug-in. Proof: standard PPI augmentation with a
cell-stratified rectifier (\texttt{ia\_ppi.py}, docstring).
\end{proposition}

\noindent \emph{Simulation} (\texttt{sim\_prop2\_coverage.py},
$\Ncal{=}800$, $1{,}500$ reps, $\mathcal{E}$ on the joint-bias
cell): marginal-PPI $95\%$ coverage falls to $\approx0.89$ as the
interaction grows and to $0.82$ under a probit judge link; the
factorial rectifier holds $0.94$--$0.95$ throughout, and also under
three variants where its working model is wrong (probit link; an
active three-way term; a quality-dependent position slope), so it is
not merely recovering its own DGP. Below $\Ncal\approx500$ the
factorial rectifier is too noisy and increases residual bias
relative to sequential correction, so it needs a few hundred gold
pairs to be worth using.

\section{Leaderboard-Impact Sketch}
\label{app:ranksweep}
A \emph{constructed} demo (\texttt{sim\_ranking\_impact.py}): six
models on a tight skill ladder, judge carrying
$\hat\imath_{PV}={+}0.15$, verbosity aligned adversarially with
rank. Real leaderboards randomise position, so the absolute
$\tau$-drop is an artefact; the ordering of methods is the point.
Sweeping $\beta_{PV}{=}{-}\beta_P$, Kendall $\tau$ to the true
order: no correction and sequential correction stay broken
($\tau{=}{+}0.20$) at every magnitude; log-odds swap-averaging and
joint $P{+}V{+}PV$ estimation stay exact; label-free IASC ranges
$\tau\in[{+}0.60,{+}0.87]$, degrading with the bias magnitude as
$\lambda_{PV}$ falls. Details and the full table in the code.
